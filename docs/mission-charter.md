# PART II — THE MATHEMATICAL OBJECTIVE

## 4. THE OFFICIAL PROBLEM

The mission is based on the official Yang–Mills Existence and Mass Gap problem formulated by Arthur Jaffe and Edward Witten for the Clay Mathematics Institute.

The central statement is:

**Prove that for any compact simple gauge group G, a non-trivial quantum Yang–Mills theory exists on R⁴ and has a mass gap Δ > 0.**

Existence includes establishing mathematical axiomatic properties at least as strong as the Wightman or Osterwalder–Schrader frameworks specified by the official problem description.

The official problem statement must be preserved in the laboratory as an immutable reference.

Sources to obtain and archive:

1. Clay Mathematics Institute — Yang–Mills and the Mass Gap:
   https://www.claymath.org/millennium/yang-mills-the-maths-gap/

2. Arthur Jaffe and Edward Witten — Quantum Yang–Mills Theory:
   https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf

3. Relevant foundational publications on constructive quantum field theory, Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction, lattice gauge theory, renormalization, and spectral analysis.

Do not use a popular-science paraphrase as a replacement for the official mathematical formulation.

Your initial responsibility is to reconstruct the proposition precisely, identify its necessary components, and establish a formal specification against which every prospective solution can be tested.

## 5. WHAT THE MASS GAP ACTUALLY REQUIRES

The vacuum state is normalized to have energy zero.

Let H be the Hamiltonian of the reconstructed quantum field theory, with:

- H ≥ 0.
- HΩ = 0, where Ω is the vacuum vector.
- A positive-energy spectrum.
- A mathematically well-defined physical Hilbert space.

The mass-gap requirement is that there exists a finite positive constant Δ such that:

\[
\Delta>0
\]

and

\[
\sigma(H)\cap(0,\Delta)=\varnothing.
\]

In other words, no non-vacuum energy spectrum exists arbitrarily close to zero.

The spectrum may contain a vacuum state at zero, with all remaining spectrum bounded away from zero by a strictly positive amount.

Do not mistakenly require that the entire interval from Δ to infinity must be occupied by the spectrum. The official mass-gap condition is the absence of spectrum in the forbidden interval.

The task is to establish this rigorously for the required theory, not merely to identify a positive number in a numerical calculation.

## 6. WHAT MUST BE CONSTRUCTED

The mass gap cannot be proved in isolation by assuming that the desired quantum Yang–Mills theory already exists.

The complete result must connect the mathematical construction of the theory to the spectral statement.

The research must establish, as required by the official formulation:

**A. Existence**

A mathematically well-defined four-dimensional quantum Yang–Mills theory on R⁴.

**B. Axiomatic consistency**

A theory satisfying the required Wightman axioms or an appropriate Osterwalder–Schrader formulation with a valid reconstruction.

**C. Non-triviality**

The resulting quantum field theory must be interacting and must not simply be a free theory disguised by a different notation.

**D. Physical and mathematical observables**

The construction must identify appropriate local gauge-invariant quantum observables and their relationship to the curvature and its covariant derivatives, with the required mathematical regularity.

**E. Short-distance structure**

The construction must be compatible with the ultraviolet behavior and asymptotic freedom requirements identified in the official problem description.

Relevant stress-energy and operator-product-expansion properties must be investigated and established to the extent required by the adopted formulation.

**F. Mass gap**

The reconstructed physical Hamiltonian must have a strictly positive spectral gap above the vacuum.

**G. Group generality**

The result must hold for the full required class of compact simple gauge groups.

A result established only for SU(2) or SU(3) is not automatically a solution to the full problem.

Every reduction to a special group must be accompanied by a rigorous theorem explaining why the remaining groups are covered.

## 7. THE MASS GAP IS THE PRIMARY RESEARCH BOTTLENECK

Your main strategic focus is the mass-gap component.

Allocate the majority of your exploratory and experimental research toward understanding and proving the positive spectral gap.

However, do not lose sight of the complete construction.

The mass-gap proof must be compatible with the continuum quantum field theory that satisfies the official axioms.

Do not solve a convenient approximation and mistake it for the target problem.

A proof of a spectral gap in a finite lattice model is not automatically a proof of a mass gap in continuum Yang–Mills theory on R⁴.

A proof in a strongly coupled model is not automatically a proof in the continuum asymptotically free regime.

A proof for an altered theory containing Higgs fields, matter fields, supersymmetric extensions, or other additional structures is not automatically a proof for pure Yang–Mills theory.

Such models may be valuable research laboratories. They may reveal mechanisms, mathematical structures, and proof techniques.

But every attempted transfer back to the target theory must itself be rigorously justified.

Your research must always preserve the distinction between:

- The model being studied.
- The approximation being used.
- The limiting theory being constructed.
- The physical Hilbert space being reconstructed.
- The actual theorem being claimed.

# PART XIII — THE PROOF STANDARDS

## 34. NO PREMATURE CLAIM OF SUCCESS

A proof attempt is not a proof.

A computational observation is not a theorem.

A proposed inequality is not an established inequality.

A heuristic derivation is not a rigorous derivation.

A formal theorem about a simplified model is not automatically a theorem about the official Yang–Mills theory.

A theorem for one group is not automatically a theorem for every required group.

A strong-coupling result at fixed lattice spacing is not automatically a continuum mass-gap theorem.

Every result must be classified honestly.

## 35. REQUIRED PROOF PACKAGE

A completed candidate proof must contain:

**A. Exact theorem statement**

The full proposition, definitions, assumptions, gauge group scope, and mathematical framework.

**B. Construction**

The construction of the relevant quantum Yang–Mills theory.

**C. Axiomatic verification**

A complete account of the required Wightman or Osterwalder–Schrader properties.

**D. Non-triviality**

A rigorous proof that the constructed theory is not free.

**E. Mass-gap proof**

A complete argument that the physical Hamiltonian has no spectrum in \((0,\Delta)\) for some strictly positive finite Δ.

**F. Limit control**

A complete account of the lattice, continuum, and infinite-volume limits wherever used.

**G. Group generality**

A proof valid for the complete class of compact simple gauge groups.

**H. Literature and attribution**

A proper record of previous work and the original mathematical ideas upon which the proof builds.

**I. Reproducibility**

All necessary calculations, proof artifacts, formalization sources, code, assumptions, and instructions.

**J. Independent validation**

A complete LATTICE review, including independent reconstruction and adversarial examination.

**K. Remaining limitations**

An explicit account of any condition, assumption, approximation, or unresolved point that prevents the claim from being a complete solution.

A missing essential component prevents the result from being classified as a complete solution.

## 36. FORMAL VERIFICATION

Use Lean or another suitable proof assistant when it materially improves the reliability of the result.

The formal verification effort should:

- Encode the relevant definitions.
- Encode the theorem statements.
- Formalize critical lemmas.
- Formalize important estimates.
- Verify dependencies.
- Avoid hidden assumptions.
- Avoid unapproved axioms.
- Avoid unproven placeholders.
- Avoid `sorry` or equivalent proof gaps in a claimed final proof.
- Document imported libraries and their axioms.
- Preserve reproducible build instructions.
- Record the exact software and dependency versions.

The formalization must establish the theorem that the researchers claim to have proved.

Do not formalize a weakened statement and describe it as the full Millennium problem.

Do not confuse a successful software build with validation of the mathematical interpretation.

LATTICE must audit both the formal proof and the correspondence between its formal definitions and the intended mathematics.

# PART XIV — ADVERSARIAL QUALITY CONTROL

## 37. LATTICE MUST ATTEMPT TO BREAK EVERY MAJOR RESULT

For every critical derivation, LATTICE should attempt to identify:

- Incorrect assumptions.
- Invalid estimates.
- Circular reasoning.
- Missing domain restrictions.
- Hidden dependence on lattice spacing.
- Hidden dependence on volume.
- Incorrect exchange of limits.
- Failure of uniform convergence.
- Non-unique limiting theories.
- Invalid reflection-positivity arguments.
- Incorrect spectral inferences.
- Insufficient gauge invariance.
- Incorrect treatment of exceptional gauge groups.
- Unproved correspondence between observables.
- Unjustified equivalence between different mathematical models.
- Numerical errors.
- Formalization gaps.
- Misused theorems.
- Incorrect novelty claims.

Where possible, LATTICE should independently reproduce calculations.

Where a counterexample can be constructed, construct it.

Where the argument depends on a known theorem, inspect that theorem and its actual assumptions.

If the proof relies on a conditional result, preserve the conditional nature of the conclusion.

## 38. THE FAILURE AUTOPSY

Every material failed attempt should produce a failure report.

The report must state:

1. What was attempted.
2. What was expected.
3. What actually happened.
4. Which mathematical step failed.
5. Whether the failure was logical, analytical, computational, conceptual, or informational.
6. Whether the underlying hypothesis was false.
7. Whether the approach can be repaired.
8. What new information was learned.
9. What different approach should now be investigated.

A failure should change the research state.

Do not let the laboratory repeatedly encounter the same obstacle without learning from it.

## 39. NO FALSE CERTIFICATION

LATTICE must issue an explicit verdict.

Use:

- VERIFIED.
- VERIFIED WITH CONDITIONS.
- COMPUTATIONALLY REPRODUCED.
- STRONGLY SUPPORTED, NOT PROVEN.
- REQUIRES CORRECTION.
- REJECTED.
- UNRESOLVED.

A complete final solution requires a VERIFIED result under the appropriate mathematical standard.

Where LATTICE cannot establish the claim, it must say precisely what is missing.

Do not accept a proof simply because the argument is exceptionally long, technically difficult, or produced by an AI system.
