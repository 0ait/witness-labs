## B. Independent reconstruction
**Interpretation.** R1-P1 asks whether "Balaban RG to the γ-exit + clustering of the effective measure" survives the 4D U(1) negative control, which Balaban results hold where, whether blocking preserves what D08.3 needs, and whether strong-coupling expansions can take over at the exit. No gap is claimed. My blind formulation (01_formulation.md §2) predicted the same two failing steps QUASAR found (no running for U(1); "g² > γ ⇒ clustering" false for U(1) in its Coulomb phase) and predicted that the θ failure could be a contour/convention artifact.

**Definitions/assumptions.** Wilson SU(N): β_W = 2N/g²; U(1): β = 1/g². X and ME1|𝒦 as in D08.3/R1-D03. θ = link reflection with the plane on block boundaries.

**Independent methods (how they differ from QUASAR's).**
- θ-intertwining: my own 4D implementation (8⁴ with L=2 corner blocks; 9⁴ with L=3 centre blocks), vectorised quaternions, three averaging rules (projection of the sum; a gauge-covariant log average relative to the straight transport; a plain log average, which my gauge-covariance check showed is *not* covariant and so was excluded), three contour orders, raw-link and gauge-invariant (plaquette and 2×1 loop) metrics, and the wrong-orientation convention as a control. Then I reran my code in QUASAR's 2D 12×12 / 3×3 geometry.
- Lemma A, Prop. 1, the H-Gap reduction: re-derived by hand (`02_reconstruction.md` R2–R4).
- Thresholds: SZZ opened at the source, conversion redone symbolically (`code/conversions_check.py`).
- Balaban: Theorem 1 hypothesis and setting read from the Project Euclid PDF excerpt via the public Tavily extract (the full PDF sits behind a bot check that I did not circumvent).
- E01: read line by line; independent checkerboard multi-hit **Metropolis** (a different algorithm from QUASAR's heat bath), measuring every sweep with Madras–Sokal τ_int.

**Core derivations.** See `02_reconstruction.md`:
- R1: time-first corner contours plus any bi-equivariant average intertwine θ modulo a coarse gauge transformation;
- R2: Lemma A and what FS82 actually supplies;
- R3: Prop. 1 with conditions;

## E. Adversarial findings
**Edge cases:**
- Even block size with a centre anchor is impossible (no centre site). Use time-first corners (R1) or odd L.
- For U(1), the large-field branch of the BIJ average (normalised sum) is *not* linear. The "U(1) passes trivially" statement holds only for the small-field argument average.

**Counterexamples (smallest first):**
- CE-005, in `07_.../counterexamples/QL-20261121-01_theta_contour_v1.md`. One SU(2) 2D configuration family (12×12, 3×3 blocks, σ=0.05) separates contour orders. Space-first corner contours fail θ by 7.0e−3. Time-first corner contours pass to 5.6e−16. This restricts Prop. 4 to its contour family.
- Non-covariant log averaging: gauge-covariance error 1.2–1.6, so it is not a block map at all.

**Alternative interpretations (QUASAR request H.1):**
- Under one reading of "crossover", only S4 discriminates. Define S6's hypothesis class by RG *history*, as measures reached by a flow that entered from below γ and keeps growing. Then U(1) never meets the hypothesis (k_* = ∞).
- But a proof of S6 on that class must then use the continued, non-perturbative growth of the coupling past γ. That is QUASAR's candidate property (i): non-abelian input at S6 under another name.
- With 𝒜_γ defined by local bounds alone, the abelian analogue contains massless Villain theories (FS82), and Lemma A bites.
- Verdict on the claim "AF is not the only place": correct if AF means *perturbative* b₀ > 0. It is semantic if "non-perturbative AF" is admitted.

**Hidden assumptions:**
- The state used in X (periodic-torus limits) versus the FS82 state (free b.c., translation-averaged subsequence). Uniqueness in the Coulomb phase is not proved in what I read.
- Prop. 1 needs infinite time extent for the transfer-matrix vector form.
- H-Gap over the whole class 𝒜_γ is strictly stronger than X. Only the closure of the RG orbit {ν_β} is needed (PROPOSAL L-14).

**Source concerns:**
- FS82 and Guth primary texts were not opened (Project Euclid bot check, APS paywall; not circumvented).
- For FS82 I read a secondary source: the README of a public Lean 4 formalization, github.com/scottnarmstrong/Frohlich-Spencer. It cites FS82 (2.89)–(2.93) and §2.11 and claims no `sorry`. Label: secondary, informal; the library was not built by me.
- arXiv:2509.19176 (abstract-level search snippet, not opened) also attributes polynomial decay at large β in d=4 to FS82.
- Balaban: excerpt level only.

**Numerical weaknesses:** D-R1P1-7. Additionally, E01 at L=8 has only two β points across the jump (Δβ = 0.01). The "near-discontinuity" is qualitative, as labelled.

**On QUASAR's request H.5 (my "computer-assisted window"):**
- My register entry L-04 is actually "rigorous small-lattice Hamiltonian gap enclosures". My check-in mislabelled the window idea as L-04; this is corrected in register 8, R-004.
- On substance, β-independence is real: one fixed problem. But the window is not small. At γ=0.1 (SU(2), 1-loop, Heuristic):
  - 146 b=2 steps to the physical crossover region (β_W ≈ 2.3);
  - a target rate m_γ ~ e^{−1/(2b₀γ)} ≈ 1.7×10⁻⁴⁷ in exit-lattice units.
- Direct computer-assisted bridging at γ=0.1 is infeasible. Feasibility hinges on how large Balaban's γ can be made rigorously (2-loop window ≈ 13.5 steps at γ=1). That is a constants problem in Balaban's programme.
