# INV001-D02 — Lattice transfer-matrix gap ⇒ lattice clustering, and passage of uniform bounds to the limit

- **Status:** (a) relies on the Established transfer-matrix results of Lüscher 1977 and Osterwalder–Seiler 1978 (properties restated below; the primary texts were not opened, and the properties are as summarised in their abstracts and secondary sources, see the literature file). (b) and (c) are elementary proofs by QUASAR and still need LATTICE verification. Label: Established theorem (standard) / proof pending verification.
- **Depends on:** D01. **Used by:** D03, report §1 (bridge theorem).

## Lattice objects (also fixed in WS03/INV-001_formulation.md)
- Λ_{L} = (ℤ/Lℤ)³ × ℤ_T is the space-time lattice of spacing a. G is a compact simple Lie group in a faithful unitary representation of dimension N. The Wilson action is S_β(U)=β Σ_p (1 − (1/N) Re Tr U_p), with β = 2N/g₀² for SU(N).
- ℋ_L := L²(G^{E_s}, Haar), where E_s is the set of spatial links of one time slice. ℋ_L^{inv} is the subspace invariant under time-zero gauge transformations G^{V_s}.
- The transfer matrix T_{β,L} (Lüscher 1977; Osterwalder–Seiler 1978) is a bounded, self-adjoint, positive operator on ℋ_L with a strictly positive continuous integral kernel. It commutes with the gauge action and the lattice symmetries, and Z = Tr(P_inv T^{N_t}) for periodic time extent N_t (P_inv the projection onto ℋ_L^{inv}). On a finite lattice the kernel is continuous on a compact space, so T is Hilbert–Schmidt; T=(T^{1/2})² with T^{1/2} Hilbert–Schmidt, so T is trace class.
- **Perron–Frobenius/Jentzsch.** A compact integral operator with strictly positive continuous kernel has a simple largest eigenvalue λ₀ with a strictly positive eigenfunction Ω_L. By uniqueness Ω_L is gauge invariant, so Ω_L∈ℋ_L^{inv}.
- **Full-sector gap:** m^{full}(β,L) := log(λ₀/λ₁^{full}), where λ₁^{full} := sup σ(T|_{ℋ_L^{inv}⊖ℂΩ_L}).
- **Local-sector gap:** let ℋ_L^{loc} be the smallest closed subspace containing Ω_L and invariant under T and under multiplication by every function of finitely many contractible spatial Wilson loops. Put m^{loc}(β,L) := log(λ₀/λ₁^{loc}) with λ₁^{loc} := sup σ(T|_{ℋ_L^{loc}⊖ℂΩ_L}). Since ℋ^{loc}⊂ℋ^{inv}, m^{loc} ≥ m^{full}. ℋ^{loc} excludes the torelon/electric-flux (centre-charged) sectors; compare E02 Part B.
- Both are in lattice units. Physical gaps: Δ^{full/loc}(a,L) := m^{full/loc}(β(a),L)/a.

## (a) Lattice clustering from a lattice gap
For bounded gauge-invariant time-zero functions F,G of links (acting on ℋ_L as multiplication operators), the ground-state connected correlation at Euclidean time separation n is
  ⟨F;G⟩_n := ⟨FΩ_L, (T/λ₀)^n GΩ_L⟩ − ⟨FΩ_L,Ω_L⟩⟨Ω_L,GΩ_L⟩.
If F,G are functions of contractible loops, then FΩ_L, GΩ_L ∈ ℋ^{loc}, and
  |⟨F;G⟩_n| ≤ ‖QFΩ_L‖‖QGΩ_L‖ e^{−m^{loc} n}.  (3)
*Proof.* T commutes with Q (Ω_L is an eigenvector) and leaves ℋ^{loc} invariant. On ℋ^{loc}⊖ℂΩ_L, ‖(T/λ₀)^n‖ = (λ₁^{loc}/λ₀)^n = e^{−m^{loc}n} by the spectral theorem for the self-adjoint positive T. Then apply Cauchy–Schwarz. ∎
For general gauge-invariant F,G, (3) holds with m^{full}.
*Periodic time.* With N_t<∞ the Euclidean expectation is a thermal trace. It differs from the ground-state correlation by terms of relative size O(e^{−m^{full}(N_t−n)}) (spectral expansion of Tr). In the bridge we take N_t→∞ first, or N_t ≥ L with exponentially small error controlled by m^{full}.

## (b) Passage of uniform bounds to the limit
Let (β_k, L_k, a_k) be a sequence with a_k→0. Let 𝒟 be a family of continuum observable labels with lattice representatives F↦F_k (for example Wilson loops, or gradient-flow-smoothed plaquettes, of fixed physical size). Assume:
- **(C1) convergence:** for every F,G∈𝒟 and t≥0, with n_k:=⌊t/a_k⌋, ⟨F_kΩ_k,(T_k/λ₀)^{n_k}G_kΩ_k⟩ → S(F,G;t) and ⟨Ω_k,F_kΩ_k⟩ → s(F).
- **(U) uniform physical lattice gap:** m^{loc}(β_k,L_k) ≥ Δ a_k for all k, with Δ>0 independent of k.

Then |S(F,G;t) − \overline{s(F)}s(G)| ≤ c(F)c(G)e^{−Δt}, where c(F)² := S(F,F;0) − |s(F)|².
*Proof.* By (3), |⟨F_k;G_k⟩_{n_k}| ≤ ‖QF_kΩ_k‖‖QG_kΩ_k‖e^{−Δa_kn_k}. Here ‖QF_kΩ_k‖² = ⟨F_kΩ_k,F_kΩ_k⟩ − |⟨Ω_k,F_kΩ_k⟩|² → c(F)² by (C1) at t=0, and a_kn_k → t. Take k→∞. ∎
(Reflection positivity also passes to the limit, since pointwise limits of positive semidefinite forms are positive semidefinite.)

## (c) Bridge theorem, lattice form (sufficiency)
Assume (C1), (U), and that S extends to a Schwinger functional satisfying (OS+), (T), (B) of D01, with 𝒟 cyclic in the reconstructed ℋ (H1). Then the reconstructed H satisfies σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅ and ker H=ℂΩ.
*Proof.* (b) gives (H2) with C(F)=c(F)²; apply D01. ∎

**Where the difficulty sits.** Steps (a)–(c) are soft. All of the content is in (U), and in (C1)+(OS)+(H1): existence of the continuum, infinite-volume limit with the required field content. (U) needs m^{loc} to be bounded below by a quantity that vanishes exactly like a_k; D03 shows it must also be O(a_k). Instead of (U), it suffices to have the weaker family-wise clustering (U′): |⟨F_k;F_k⟩_{n}| ≤ C(F)e^{−Δa_kn} for all n, k and F∈𝒟. This is weaker because it need not hold on all of ℋ^{loc}.
