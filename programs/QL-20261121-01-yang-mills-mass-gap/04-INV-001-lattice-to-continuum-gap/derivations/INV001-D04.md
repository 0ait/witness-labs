# INV001-D04 — Finite-volume gaps are neither sufficient nor necessary; uniform gaps exclude non-clustering limits

- **Status:** Examples A and B are exact computations (Established). Lemma D04.2 is QUASAR's own elementary proof (pending LATTICE verification). E03 provides numerical illustration. **Depends on:** D01, D02.

## Example A (gap closes exponentially; pure phases trivially gapped) — rigid-column Ising strip
Configurations s_{t}∈{±1} for t∈ℤ, interpreting each time slice as a column of L spins forced equal (a column-rigid strip), with nearest-neighbour coupling K>0 between slices. The weight is exp(LK Σ_t s_t s_{t+1}). The transfer matrix is T_L = [[e^{LK}, e^{−LK}],[e^{−LK}, e^{LK}]], with eigenvalues λ₀=2cosh(LK) (vector (1,1)) and λ₁=2sinh(LK) (vector (1,−1)), so
  m_lat(L) = log(λ₀/λ₁) = log coth(LK) = 2e^{−2LK}(1+O(e^{−4LK})) → 0.
The time-direction correlation ⟨s_0s_t⟩=tanh(LK)^{|t|} → 1 as L→∞. The limit state is ½(δ_{+}+δ_{−}): it does not cluster. Each pure phase δ_± has no fluctuations at all, so its connected correlations vanish identically and its reconstructed Hilbert space is ℂΩ. So the finite-volume gap closes while each pure infinite-volume phase is (trivially) gapped. E03 checks this to 60 digits (ratio to 2e^{−2LK} → 1). The transverse-field Ising chain in E03 is a non-trivial instance: splitting ~ (h/J)^L while the gap to genuine excitations stays ≈4(J−h).

## Example B (gap positive for every L, zero in the limit) — massless lattice field, twisted BC
A free real scalar on the spatial torus (ℤ/Lℤ)^d, d≥2 (to avoid the infrared problems of the massless field in 1+1 dimensions), with antiperiodic boundary conditions, continuous time, lattice Laplacian, zero bare mass. One-particle energies are ω(p)=2(Σ_i sin²(p_i/2))^{1/2}, p∈(2π/L)(ℤ+½)^d. Then the finite-volume gap is ω_min(L)=2√d·sin(π/(2L))>0 for every L, and ω_min(L)→0. The infinite-volume Hamiltonian has one-particle spectrum {ω(p):p∈[−π,π]^d}=[0,2√d]. Fock sums of these cover [0,∞), so σ(H)=[0,∞) and there is no gap. A strictly positive gap at every finite L therefore carries no information about L=∞.

## Lemma D04.2 (uniform finite-volume gap ⇒ clustering ⇒ unique vacuum)
Let ω_L be lattice ground-state functionals (from Ω_L) converging, for a family 𝒟 of local observables at all time separations, to a limit S. If m^{loc}(L) ≥ m > 0 for all L in the sequence (fixed lattice spacing), then for all F∈𝒟, S(F,F;t)−|s(F)|² ≤ c(F)²e^{−mt}. Consequently:
- (i) the reconstruction from 𝒟 has ker H=ℂΩ and σ(H)∩(0,m)=∅ (by D01, cyclicity holding by construction);
- (ii) S cannot be a mixture ½(ω₊+ω₋) of two time-clustering states with ω₊(F)≠ω₋(F) for some F∈𝒟.

*Proof.* The bound is D02(b) with a fixed spacing. For (ii): if S=½(ω₊+ω₋) with each ω_± clustering in time, then S(F,F;t)−|s(F)|² → ½|f₊|²+½|f₋|²−¼|f₊+f₋|² = ¼|f₊−f₋|² > 0 as t→∞, contradicting the exponential bound. ∎
*Contrapositive (finite-volume closure is forced by vacuum degeneracy):* if the infinite-volume limit is such a mixture, then liminf_L m^{loc}(L)=0.

## Consequences for YM
1. A finite-volume gap *uniform in L* for the full gauge-invariant sector is **not necessary** for an infinite-volume gap. On a torus, ℋ^{inv} contains centre-charged (Polyakov-loop / 't Hooft electric-flux / torelon) sectors whose energies are volume-dependent: ≈σ·ℓ in a confining regime, and possibly very small in small or intermediate volumes. E02 Part B shows this in the Z₂ ladder: at J/h≥8 the full gap is the torelon energy σ(J)·n, far below the local gap, for every n≤16 computed.
2. The quantity relevant to the bridge is m^{loc} (or the gap above a pure-phase vacuum), not m^{full}.
3. A uniform-in-L bound on m^{loc} at fixed β does give clustering and a unique vacuum in the lattice infinite-volume limit (D04.2). This is the precise sense of Jaffe–Witten's remark that "a uniform gap for finite-volume approximations may play a fundamental role in the proof of existence of the infinite-volume limit". (Uniqueness of the limit needs more than (i): it needs convergence along the whole sequence, which clustering helps to establish through standard Ruelle/Dobrushin-type arguments not reproduced here.)
