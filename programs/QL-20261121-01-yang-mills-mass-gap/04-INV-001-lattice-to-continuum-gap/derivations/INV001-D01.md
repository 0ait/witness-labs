# INV001-D01 — Bridge lemma (sufficiency): uniform exponential clustering of a cyclic family ⇒ σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅

- **Status:** the statement is a standard fact of OS reconstruction plus spectral theory (knowledge label: **Established theorem**). The proof below is QUASAR's own and still needs LATTICE's independent verification. It is not attributed to a specific theorem number: secondary sources point to Glimm–Jaffe, *Quantum Physics* (2nd ed., 1987), Ch. 6, but that book was not opened (paywalled), so no theorem number is claimed.
- **Workstreams:** WS01, WS06. Depends on nothing else in INV-001. **Used by:** D02, D03, D04, D06, and the report §1.

## Setting
Let 𝒜₊ be a *-algebra of "positive-time" observables, for example polynomials in smeared local gauge-invariant fields supported in {x⁰>0}. Let θ be time reflection, τ_t (t≥0) time translation with τ_t𝒜₊⊂𝒜₊, and S a linear functional on the algebra generated by 𝒜₊∪θ𝒜₊ (the limiting Schwinger functional) such that:

- **(OS+)** reflection positivity: S(θ(F̄)F) ≥ 0 for all F∈𝒜₊.
- **(T)** τ_t-invariance, and continuity: t ↦ S(θ(F̄)·τ_tG) is continuous on [0,∞) for F,G∈𝒜₊.
- **(B)** boundedness: |S(θ(F̄)τ_tF)| ≤ c_F for all t≥0. This holds automatically for bounded observables, or follows from (OS+) via the semigroup argument in [OS73/75].

**OS reconstruction (standard).** Let ℋ be the completion of 𝒜₊/𝒩 under ⟨F,G⟩:=S(θ(F̄)G), where 𝒩 is the null space; write F̂ for the class of F and Ω:=1̂. Then P(t)F̂:=(τ_tF)^ is well defined and extends to a strongly continuous, self-adjoint, contraction semigroup with P(t)Ω=Ω. Hence P(t)=e^{−tH} with H=H*≥0 and HΩ=0, and
  ⟨F̂, e^{−tH}Ĝ⟩ = S(θ(F̄)·τ_tG).  (1)
(Self-adjointness of P(t) uses reflection symmetry θτ_t=τ_{−t}θ. The contraction property uses the Schwarz inequality iterated: ‖P(t)ψ‖² ≤ ‖P(2^n t)ψ‖^{2^{1−n}}‖ψ‖^{2−2^{1−n}} together with (B).)

Define the connected correlation C_{F,G}(t) := S(θ(F̄)τ_tG) − S(θ(F̄))S(G) and Q := 1 − |Ω⟩⟨Ω|. Because e^{−tH}Ω=Ω,
  C_{F,G}(t) = ⟨QF̂, e^{−tH} QĜ⟩.  (2)
(Proof: expand ⟨F̂,e^{−tH}Ĝ⟩ with F̂=⟨Ω,F̂⟩Ω+QF̂. The cross terms vanish because ⟨Ω,e^{−tH}QĜ⟩=⟨e^{−tH}Ω,QĜ⟩=⟨Ω,QĜ⟩=0, and S(θ(F̄))=⟨F̂,Ω⟩.)

## Lemma D01
Let 𝒟⊂𝒜₊ satisfy:
- **(H1, cyclicity)** {F̂ : F∈𝒟} is dense in ℋ.
- **(H2, uniform-rate clustering)** there is a single Δ>0 such that for every F∈𝒟 there is C(F)<∞ with |C_{F,F}(t)| ≤ C(F)e^{−Δt} for all t ≥ t_F, where t_F ≥ 0 may depend on F.

Then E_H([0,Δ)) = |Ω⟩⟨Ω|, where E_H is the spectral measure of H. Equivalently: σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅, and ker H = ℂΩ (the vacuum is unique).

## Proof
1. Fix F∈𝒟 and put ψ:=QF̂. Let ν(·):=⟨ψ,E_H(·)ψ⟩, a finite positive Borel measure on [0,∞) with ν([0,∞))=‖ψ‖². **This is where the spectral theorem enters:** by functional calculus, ⟨ψ,e^{−tH}ψ⟩ = ∫_{[0,∞)}e^{−tλ}dν(λ). By (2) this equals C_{F,F}(t), a non-negative number.
2. Fix δ∈(0,Δ). For t≥t_F: ν([0,Δ−δ])·e^{−t(Δ−δ)} ≤ ∫_{[0,Δ−δ]}e^{−tλ}dν ≤ ∫e^{−tλ}dν = C_{F,F}(t) ≤ C(F)e^{−Δt}. Hence ν([0,Δ−δ]) ≤ C(F)e^{−δt} for all t≥t_F. Letting t→∞ gives ν([0,Δ−δ])=0. Taking δ↓0 along a sequence and using countable additivity, ν([0,Δ))=0, i.e. ‖E_H([0,Δ))QF̂‖² = 0.
3. Hence the bounded operator B:=E_H([0,Δ))Q vanishes on the set {F̂:F∈𝒟}. By (H1) and continuity of B, B=0 on ℋ. Linearity of 𝒟 is not needed: vanishing on a dense subset is enough.
4. Since H commutes with Q (Ω is an eigenvector of the self-adjoint H, so its span and orthocomplement are invariant), E_H([0,Δ)) = E_H([0,Δ))(|Ω⟩⟨Ω|+Q) = |Ω⟩⟨Ω|, using E_H([0,Δ))Ω=Ω because HΩ=0. Therefore no spectral subspace other than ℂΩ lies in [0,Δ): σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅ and dim ker H=1. ∎

## Converse (shows the lemma is sharp)
If σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅ and ker H=ℂΩ, then for all F,G∈𝒜₊ and t≥0, |C_{F,G}(t)| ≤ ‖QF̂‖‖QĜ‖e^{−Δt}. Proof: ‖e^{−tH}Q‖ = sup_{λ∈σ(H|_{QH})}e^{−tλ} ≤ e^{−Δt}, then apply Cauchy–Schwarz to (2). So the gap is *equivalent* to uniform-rate exponential clustering of a cyclic family, with the explicit constant C(F,G)=‖QF̂‖‖QĜ‖.

## Role of each hypothesis
- **(H1) cyclicity.** Without it, the argument only gives E_H([0,Δ))Q=0 on the closed subspace 𝒦 spanned by 𝒟Ω, i.e. a gap for the part of H that 𝒟 can "see". Counterexamples where 𝒦≠ℋ and H has spectrum in (0,Δ) on 𝒦^⊥ are in D06. If ℋ is *defined* as the reconstruction from 𝒟 itself, (H1) holds by construction. It then becomes a real issue only when ℋ has to be the Hilbert space of a larger field content, e.g. the Wightman space generated by all local gauge-invariant composites (Tr F², …) required by Jaffe–Witten.
- **(H2) uniformity of Δ.** Δ must be one number for the whole family. If each F decays at its own rate Δ(F)>0 with inf_FΔ(F)=0, the conclusion fails. Example: H=multiplication by λ on L²([0,∞),dλ), plus ℂΩ. The vectors supported in [ε,∞) for some ε>0 form a dense set, each decays like e^{−εt}, yet σ(H)=[0,∞). In the lattice→continuum context, uniformity is also needed in the regulators: Δ must not depend on (a,L) (D02, D03).
- **C(F) may depend on F.** The proof kills C(F) with e^{−δt} at fixed F as t→∞, so no uniformity in F is needed. A polynomial prefactor C(F)(1+t)^k also suffices, since (1+t)^ke^{−δt}→0. Only the exponential rate must be uniform.
- **Only the diagonal F=G is needed** for the conclusion. Off-diagonal bounds follow afterwards from the converse.
- **Where the spectral measure is used:** only in step 1 (Laplace-transform representation of ⟨ψ,e^{−tH}ψ⟩) and step 2 (a Chebyshev/Markov-type lower bound of a Laplace transform by the mass of [0,Δ−δ]).

## Remarks
1. With Lorentz covariance (Wightman reconstruction), σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅ on Ω^⊥ is equivalent to the mass spectrum on Ω^⊥ lying in [Δ,∞). A state of mass μ<Δ at rest has energy μ; conversely H≥(P²+M²)^{1/2}≥M. The Jaffe–Witten definition is the H-statement.
2. Time direction versus space direction: (H2) is a time-direction statement. On the lattice, the transfer matrix gives exactly that direction (D02). Exponential decay in spatial directions transfers to time only through Euclidean (or at least hypercubic) invariance of the limit.
