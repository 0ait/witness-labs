# INV001-D06 — Neither one observable's decay nor a gap on one sector gives the full gap

- **Status:** exact counterexamples (Established, standard). Proofs are QUASAR's (pending verification). **Depends on:** D01.

## D06.1 (a single observable can overestimate the gap) — Wick square
In the free scalar field of mass m (D≥3), consider F = :φ²:(f) with f a time-smeared test function. Its connected two-point function is 2⟨φφ⟩², which decays at rate 2m, while the gap is m. Proof: Wick's theorem gives ⟨:φ²:(x):φ²:(y)⟩ = 2G(x−y)², and G decays like e^{−m|x−y|}·poly. In spectral terms, the vector F̂ lies in the even (two-particle and higher) Fock sector, whose spectrum starts at 2m. The general mechanism: if every observable measured is invariant under a symmetry (here φ→−φ), only the invariant sector is seen. **YM reading:** decay of plaquette–plaquette correlations measures the lowest state in the channel the plaquette couples to (0⁺⁺, centre-neutral, zero momentum). It does not bound states in other J^{PC} channels, so the true gap could be smaller than the 0⁺⁺ mass.

## D06.2 (a gap on one sector says nothing about the rest)
Let K be a group of unitaries with KΩ=Ω commuting with H. If 𝒟 consists of K-invariant observables, then the closure of 𝒟Ω lies in ℋ^K (the K-invariant vectors). D01 then yields only E_H([0,Δ))Q|_{ℋ^K}=0. Example: the product theory φ₁(mass m)⊗φ₂(massless), with K the reflection φ₂→−φ₂ and 𝒟 = polynomials in φ₁ only. Every F∈𝒟 clusters at rate m, yet H=H₁⊗1+1⊗H₂ has σ(H)=[0,∞) because σ(H₂)=[0,∞). This example also shows a single observable family can be OS-positive and exponentially clustering while H is gapless (failure of (H1)).

## D06.3 (decay of each observable at its own rate is not enough)
This is the (H2) remark of D01: on ℋ=ℂΩ⊕L²([0,∞),dλ) with H=0⊕λ, the vectors with spectral support in [ε,∞) for some ε>0 are dense and each clusters at rate ε, yet σ(H)=[0,∞).

## D06.4 (finite-volume sectors) — see D04
On a lattice torus, a gap in ℋ^{loc} does not bound the torelon/electric-flux sectors, and conversely those sectors can make m^{full} tiny without affecting the infinite-volume local gap (E02 Part B).

## Consequence for a bridge theorem
The family 𝒟 must be (i) cyclic for the *physical* Hilbert space (all local gauge-invariant composites, not just one channel), and (ii) uniformly clustering at one rate Δ. A proof that controls one correlator, e.g. the plaquette two-point function, or one symmetry channel, does not satisfy the hypotheses of D01.
