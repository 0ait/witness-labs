## 1. The bridge theorem (precise target)
Objects (WS03 formulation §1):
- the Wilson measure μ_{β,L} on Λ_L = (ℤ/Lℤ)³×ℤ;
- the transfer matrix T_{β,L} (positive, self-adjoint, trace class);
- the local sector ℋ^{loc}_L generated by contractible Wilson loops;
- m^{loc}(β,L) = −log(λ₁^{loc}/λ₀) in lattice units;
- a renormalisation trajectory a(β)→0 fixed by a *non-gap* condition;
- Δ_lat(β,L) = m^{loc}(β,L)/a(β).

**Bridge Theorem (target; Open problem).** There exist a(β), a family 𝒟 of renormalised local gauge-invariant observables, and Δ>0, β₀, L₀(β) such that:
- (i) **[C1⁺]** the Schwinger functions of 𝒟 converge along every (β_k,L_k) with β_k→∞ and L_k a(β_k)→∞, to a unique, non-trivial limit satisfying OS0–OS3;
- (ii) **[U]** m^{loc}(β,L) ≥ Δ·a(β) for all β≥β₀ and L≥L₀(β), or the family-wise version (U′);
- (iii) **[H1]** 𝒟 is cyclic in the physical (Jaffe–Witten) Hilbert space.

The conclusion is σ(H)∩(0,Δ)=∅ with a unique vacuum.

The **implication** (i)+(ii)+(iii) ⇒ gap is proved (D01 + D02). The **hypotheses** are not known for 4D non-abelian G, and each is an open problem.

## 4. Literature: what exists and why it does not supply (U) or (C1⁺)
(Details and verified quotes in `INV-001_literature.md`; all DOIs Crossref-checked.)
- **Transfer matrix/positivity** (Lüscher 1977 S-101; OS78 S-005; Menotti–Pelissetto S-102): give the lattice objects and D02(a) at each fixed β,L. They say nothing uniform in β.
- **Strong coupling.**
  - OS78/Seiler S-105: lattice mass gap for small β.
  - SZZ 2022 (S-106): Thm 1.2 unique infinite-volume measure; Thm 1.4 LSI; Cor 1.6 exponential decay for |β| < 1/(16(d−1)).
  - SZZ-YMH S-107; Nissim S-016; CNS area law S-108/S-109; Durhuus–Fröhlich S-110.
  - **Why insufficient.** All hold only for β ≤ β_max(d,N), a fixed region away from the continuum limit β→∞. Their gaps are in lattice units, uniform in L but not in β, so (U) is untouched (D03). Area law is not a gap (S-108/109 prove area law, not (U)).
  - Adhikari–Cao S-111 (finite G, d=4, β ≥ (114+4log|G|)/Δ_G, rate (β/2)Δ_G): weak coupling, but the lattice-unit rate *grows* with β. By D03.2 it is consistent with the triviality of finite-group continuum limits, and it is not a continuum gap.
- **Balaban (S-006/7, S-112…S-119; exposition Dimock S-120).**
  - UV stability of 4D lattice YM: bounds on the partition function/effective actions uniform in the lattice spacing on a finite torus. The 1989-II Theorem 1 is conditional on effective couplings in ]0,γ] with γ small (verified quote).
  - **Why insufficient.** No correlation functions or Schwinger-function limits (C1⁺), no infinite volume, no control once the running coupling leaves ]0,γ], i.e. exactly at the scale where a gap must appear (the crossover; D08.3).
- **Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor S-121.** SU(2) YM₄ with an infrared cutoff (axial gauge). The IR cutoff is the regime where a gap must be *proved*; it is assumed away.
- **Chatterjee S-122.** Formulates the lattice mass gap (Problem 5.1) and continuum limit (5.2) as open problems.
- **Stochastic quantisation** (CCHS 2D S-123; Chevyrev–Shen S-124 2D invariant measure; CCHS 3D YMH S-125). 2D/3D, with the measure's invariance or global existence open in 3D. No 4D and no gap.
- **Glimm–Jaffe S-126.** OS reconstruction framework (D01). Not opened; no theorem number claimed.
- **Abelian obstruction** (Guth S-127; Fröhlich–Spencer S-128; Established for U(1), texts not opened).
  - 4D U(1) LGT is gapped at strong coupling and massless at weak coupling. Any method that would equally apply to U(1) cannot prove ME1, so non-abelian asymptotic freedom must enter.
  - This is also why strong-coupling gaps (§4 above) cannot be continued to β→∞ by soft arguments.
- **2026 claimed existence construction** (parallel audit; S-013/S-014; *claim*, first-pass audit only, not rebuilt). The assessment states it constructs subsequential Wightman limits and "does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap".
  - Its recorded internal spectral bound is polynomial: ⟨A,(e^{−sH}−P₀)A⟩ ≤ M s^{−2}, i.e. weight of (0,u] ≤ M u².
  - By D01, this cannot yield a gap. The missing estimate becomes ME2′ (§5). Not relied upon anywhere in INV-001.

## 5. The precise missing estimates (quantified)
Let a(β) be fixed by a non-gap renormalisation condition (e.g. a finite-volume running coupling ḡ²(ℓ_ref/a) = u₀).
- **ME1 (uniform physical gap).** ∃Δ>0 ∃β₀ ∀β≥β₀ ∃L₀(β) ∀L≥L₀(β): m^{loc}(β,L) ≥ Δ·a(β).
  - Weaker sufficient form (U′): ∃Δ>0 ∀F∈𝒟 ∃C(F) ∀β≥β₀, L≥L₀(β), n≥0: |⟨θF_β; τ_nF_β⟩_{β,L}| ≤ C(F)e^{−Δa(β)n}.
  - Equivalent scale-free form: liminf_{β→∞} inf_{L≥L₀(β)} m^{loc}(β,L)/μ_lat(β) > 0 for the chosen scale μ_lat.
- **ME2 (convergence and field content).** For all n and F₁..F_n∈𝒟: lim S^{(n)}_{β_k,L_k}(F₁,…,F_n) exists along every admissible sequence, is independent of it, and satisfies OS0–OS3, including restoration of O(4) invariance. It must be non-Gaussian, and 𝒟 must be cyclic for a theory with the Jaffe–Witten local-field content.
- **ME2′ (gap statement relative to the 2026 claimed construction, if verified).** For every phase/limit point T and every A in a family whose vectors are cyclic in T's Hilbert space: ⟨A,(e^{−sH}−P₀)A⟩ ≤ M_A e^{−Δs} for s ≥ 1, with Δ>0 independent of A and of the phase/subsequence. Equivalently, the spectral weight of (0,Δ) vanishes. This upgrades the recorded M s^{−2} bound. The proved implication ME2′ ⇒ gap is D01.
- **ME3 (upper window, needed for non-triviality; proved necessary in D03.2).** ∃C ∀β≥β₀: inf_L m^{loc}(β,L) ≤ C·a(β).
- **ME-R1 (crossover clustering = hypothesis X of D08.3).** ∃a_*>0, m_*>0, C(F') ∀β≥β₀ ∀L≥L₀(β) ∀F'∈𝒮' ∀n: 0 ≤ ⟨θ'F';τ'_nF'⟩_{μ'_{β,L}} ≤ C(F')e^{−m_*n}, where μ'_{β,L} = (B_β)_*μ_{β,L} is the block-spin measure at scale a_*. By D08.3, ME-R1 + cyclicity of 𝒮' ⇒ ME1.
- **ME-R2 (scale-uniform functional inequality).** For the block-spin measures μ'_{β,L} at scale a_*: a Poincaré (or LSI) inequality for a coarse-local, reversible dynamics, Var_{μ'}(F) ≤ κ^{−1}ℰ'(F,F) with κ>0 independent of β and L. Together with a Dobrushin/SZZ-type "dynamical gap ⇒ spatial decay" argument this gives ME-R1. (E04 shows the bare-measure BE route is void at weak coupling.)

## 6. Ranked attack routes (each has a first derivation or experiment)
1. **R1. Multiscale RG to the crossover scale + strong-coupling clustering of the effective measure.**
   - Reduction: D08.3 (done: the conditional reduction ME-R1 ⇒ ME1). 2D sanity: E01 decimation.
   - Next step: extend a strong-coupling clustering theorem (SZZ/Seiler) from Wilson actions to a β-uniform class of non-local effective actions containing Balaban's effective actions at the scale where g_k² first exceeds γ.
   - The obstacle is the crossover window γ < g_k² < g_strong², where no tool applies.
   - Ranked first because it is the only route that combines existing rigorous control (Balaban UV; strong-coupling IR).
2. **R2. Scale-uniform functional inequalities (Poincaré/LSI) for block-spin measures.**
   - First experiment: E04. Bare single-link BE is void for β≥2, while the true gap ≈ β+½. This shows the curvature criterion fails for structural reasons (non-convexity at the antipode, measure ~e^{−2β}), not because the gap is small.
   - Next step: a 2D test of ME-R2 using the exact decimated measures of E01. Compute a Poincaré constant of a coarse heat-bath dynamics vs β at fixed a_*, then test a 3D Z₂ or U(1) analogue numerically.
3. **R5. Finite-size mixing criteria (Dobrushin–Shlosman / Martinelli–Olivieri type) for WS04.**
   - First derivation: D04.2 (uniform finite-volume local gap ⇒ clustering/unique vacuum) and D08.1 (progression decay suffices).
   - Next step: formulate a criterion "a gap estimate on one finite block of physical size ℓ_* at all β≥β₀ ⇒ (U)". The obstacle is that such criteria are proved for finite-range lattice spin systems with bounded single-site spaces; gauge invariance and the β-dependent block size L_* = ℓ_*/a(β) break uniformity. Numerically, E02-B shows block (ladder) local gaps and their torelon contamination.
4. **R3. Hamiltonian coercivity in the Kogut–Susskind formulation.**
   - First experiment: E02 (single-plaquette gap 3.27 at g²=1 → 4 as g→0, in lattice units; ladder local gaps).
   - Next step: a lower bound H_KS^{loc} ≥ c·(g²/a)·… with explicit control of the g-dependence. By D03 it must degrade exactly like a(g); that dependence is non-perturbative and is the obstacle.
5. **R4. Dimensional-reduction (Durhuus–Fröhlich) σ-model comparison.** Speculative (Heuristic). S-109 uses DF80 only for area law, which is not a gap. No first step beyond the literature reading.
